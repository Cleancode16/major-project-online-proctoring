A.3 SAMPLING (V2)
-----------------
v1: every 10th frame, stop after MAX_FRAMES = 50 processed frames
    (= 1.7 s of a 30 fps video ... and the stop never triggers on short
    clips; a 1 h exam would be analysed for its first ~17 s only).
v2: time based. sampling.fps = 3.0 samples/s of video time over the whole
    video (sampling.max_samples = None). A frame is taken when its
    timestamp t = frame_index / native_fps reaches the next sample time
    (k / 3.0 s). Skipped frames are grab()'ed, not decoded.
    Each sample carries t (seconds), used by every later stage, so
    persistence and durations are in seconds, independent of camera fps.
    Live mode: the UI calls VideoAnalyzer.process() at the same rate.
DET2 (user report: phones and multiple persons not detected accurately).
    Looked at the live session 75_20261015_001107 (run before PERF1: 0.7
    samples/s): the room was dark (mean grey ~45/255); the one "multiple
    persons" event was FALSE (the candidate's arm holding a dark, blurred
    phone counted as a 2nd person; the phone itself was missed); gaze was
    "away" 98% because calibration had 2 samples reading -48 deg.
    Changes: (1) persons.attach_frac 0.3: a face-less person box that
    overlaps a face-bearing person box by >= 30% of its own area is that
    person's arm / hand (detectors.drop_attached); all 6 real two-person
    snapshots of earlier sessions still count 2. (2) objects: boxes inside
    the face box are ignored only below conf 0.5
    (ignore_inside_face_max_conf), so a phone held up in front of the face
    counts. (3) L2CS calibration gate: calibration gaze must be within
    |h| <= 25, |v| <= 35 deg absolute and >= 3 such samples; otherwise the
    forward gaze is taken from the first 5 frontal analysis samples (gaze
    not flagged until then). (4) Low light: per-sample brightness (mean
    grey of a 64x36 thumbnail); live warning "low_light" when the 3 s mean
    is < 60, report warning LOW_LIGHT when >= 30% of samples are dark.
    Not changed, with reasons: YOLOv8m (no better on the snapshots, 343 vs
    201 ms); CLAHE / gamma brightening (raised a 2nd person 0.30 -> 0.50 but
    lowered the candidate's own person conf and added a false "tv").
    Evidence snapshots carry drawn boxes, so they cannot score phone
    recall; the labelled sample clip (normal light) has phone recall 1.0.
FPS10 (user request "take 10 fps to detect all"): sampling.fps = 10.
    Recorded videos are analysed at 10 samples/s (3.3x the samples of 3 fps;
    at ~300 ms per sample with L2CS + YOLOv8s that is ~3 s of compute per
    second of video, so a recorded job takes about 3x the video length).
    Live: the session hands the newest frame every 0.1 s, frames that
    arrive while the analyzer is busy are dropped, so the live rate is
    whatever the CPU reaches (~2.5-3 samples/s on the dev machine after
    PERF1); 10 fps live needs ~100 ms per sample (a GPU or lighter models).
    All sample-count windows are now derived from seconds at the actual
    rate (video/pipeline.windows): head-pose / gaze smoothing smooth_s 1 s,
    identity majority vote vote_s 1.6 s, person gap persist_gap_s 0.33 s,
    static-face track static_s 3 s, objects "k of n" over persist_window_s
    1 s with k = max(2, ceil(0.4 n)) (2 of 3 at 3 fps, 4 of 10 at 10 fps),
    weak-object reach weak_reach_s 0.33 s. At 3 fps they equal the old
    sample counts exactly; the legacy profile keeps its counts. Live
    sessions re-measure the rate (after 5 samples, when it differs by
    > 15%) and resize the causal smoothers and the 1 s live debounce, so a
    "10 fps" setting running at 3 fps does not get 3x longer windows.
    max_thumbnails 400 -> 1200 for the extra samples.
Cost (measured on the sample clip, 1920x1080, CPU, median after warm-up,
YOLOv8s): face detect + FaceNet 64 ms, face mesh + pose + gaze 11 ms,
YOLO 95 ms, total ~172 ms per sample -> 3 fps needs ~0.52 s of compute per
second of video. FaceNet runs as a compiled tf.function (37.5 ms/face vs
236 ms for an eager call; outputs identical, max abs diff 0.0).


A.4 CALIBRATION (V3)
--------------------
v1: mean of the first 3 processed frames, whoever they show and wherever
    they look.
v2: window = first calibration.duration_s = 3.0 s of samples.
    A sample is ACCEPTED into the baseline only if
      - a face is visible and face mesh succeeded,
      - identity verified as the enrolled person (require_identity), and
      - it is frontal enough: |yaw| <= 25 and |pitch| <= 25 (absolute,
        after the V1 fix, so this gate is meaningful).
    Baseline = per-axis MEDIAN of accepted samples (robust to outliers;
    calibration.aggregate). Also stores median gaze ratios (used by §A.6).
    Ends when >= 3.0 s elapsed AND >= min_samples = 5 accepted; if that is
    not reached the window extends up to max_duration_s = 10 s, then uses
    whatever was accepted (>= 1). If nothing passed the gates by then
    (e.g. a side-mounted camera makes every frame read |yaw| > 25), the
    baseline falls back to the median of ALL samples that had a pose and
    the report carries calibration.fallback = true. Only if no face pose
    was seen at all does the baseline stay unset; then pose/gaze are not
    flagged and the report says so.
    All samples inside the window have phase = "calibration"; they are
    excluded from HEAD_POSE/GAZE counting only (§A.10).


A.5 HEAD POSE
-------------
Problem in v1: the 3D face model uses y-up / z-towards-camera, but solvePnP
works in the OpenCV camera frame (x right, y DOWN, z AWAY from camera). The
recovered rotation therefore contains a 180 deg flip about x, so a frontal
face gives pitch near +-180 deg (measured on the sample clip: baseline pitch
-168.2 deg). Small nods then jump between +179 and -179 (wraparound).

Fix (config head_pose.euler = "fixed"):
  1. 3D model expressed in camera convention: (x, -y, -z) of the v1 model.
     A frontal face now gives R ~ I and yaw/pitch/roll ~ 0.
  2. 2D landmark points kept as floats (v1 truncated to int pixels).
  3. Euler decomposition for R = Rz(roll) * Ry(yaw) * Rx(pitch):
        pitch = atan2(R[2,1], R[2,2])
        yaw   = atan2(-R[2,0], sqrt(R[0,0]^2 + R[1,0]^2))
        roll  = atan2(R[1,0], R[0,0])
     Sign convention of the output (verified by tests/test_v1_head_pose.py):
        yaw   > 0  face turned towards IMAGE RIGHT
        pitch > 0  face tilted DOWN (chin towards chest)
        roll  > 0  head tilted clockwise in the image
  4. All relative angles use wrap180(angle - baseline), so values stay in
     [-180, 180) even near the boundary.
Validation: synthetic projection test (known rotations -> project model ->
solvePnP -> recovered angles within 1 deg) + frontal frames of the sample
clip must give |pitch| < 15 deg.
Known residual: with the generic 6-point model, a frontal face on the sample
clip reads yaw ~ -14 deg, pitch ~ +11 deg (model/camera-intrinsics mismatch,
constant per person and camera). The calibration baseline removes it; only
relative angles are thresholded.

Temporal rules (V4):
  rel = wrap180(angle - baseline) per axis, then a MEDIAN over
  head_pose.smooth_window = 3 samples (removes one-sample spikes from
  blinks or landmark jitter). Final counts use a CENTRED median (zero
  lag; computed at finalize). The live UI can only use a causal median,
  which shifts each flagged run 1 sample late, so it is used for the live
  display only.
  raw deviation = |rel_yaw| > 15 OR |rel_pitch| > 10 OR |rel_roll| > 10
  A deviation counts only if it persists head_pose.persist_s = 1.0 s
  (= ceil(1.0 * sample_fps) consecutive samples, 3 at 3 fps). Once confirmed,
  the whole run is flagged from its first sample (offline filter_runs at
  finalize; the live UI uses a causal version that lags by persist_s).

Accuracy upgrade (HP2, 2026-10-14; head_pose.method = "template"):
  1. Person-specific rigid template. During calibration, every accepted
     sample also stores the candidate's own MediaPipe 3D landmarks
     (X = x*w, Y = y*h, Z = z*w; same axes as the camera frame) for
     head_pose.rigid_ids = 33 points on bone-anchored areas: forehead /
     temples (10, 109, 67, 103, 54, 338, 297, 332, 284), nose bridge and
     tip (151, 9, 8, 168, 6, 197, 195, 5, 4, 1, 98, 327), eye corners
     (33, 133, 362, 263), cheekbones / face sides (234, 454, 127, 356, 93,
     323, 116, 345). Mouth, jaw, eyebrows and eyelids are excluded because
     they move with expressions and speech. Each set is centred and scaled
     to unit RMS radius; template = per-coordinate median.
  2. Every frame: the same points, centred + scaled, are aligned to the
     template with the Kabsch / Umeyama least-squares rotation (SVD,
     reflection-corrected). The relative angles come straight from that
     rotation via euler_from_matrix (same sign convention as above), so
     no generic-face bias and no subtraction of a PnP baseline is needed.
     33 points instead of 6 makes the estimate far less sensitive to one
     jittery landmark. PnP (generic model) is still used for the absolute
     "frontal enough" calibration gate.
     Fallback: no template (no accepted calibration landmarks) -> v2 PnP
     relative angles as before.
  3. Hysteresis: a deviation starts when an axis exceeds its threshold
     and ends only when every axis is back below threshold - hyst_deg
     (3 deg), so one long turn is not split into several events by noise
     around the threshold.
  4. Slow baseline adaptation (posture drift in long exams): offset +=
     (rel - offset) * dt / adapt_tau_s (120 s), applied only on samples
     whose |rel - offset| < thr / 2 on every axis (clearly normal), and
     |offset| is capped at adapt_max_deg (8 deg) per axis, so a slow
     deliberate turn can never be absorbed into the baseline.
     rel_* stored in the samples are after the offset; raw values are kept
     as raw_rel_*.
  Validation: tests/test_hp2_pose_gaze.py (synthetic rotations of a 3D
  point set; NOT executed yet - the user runs the tests).


A.6 GAZE (V5)
-------------
v1 bug (measured): ratio = (iris_x - outer_x) / |inner_x - outer_x| for both
eyes. For the eye on the image-right side the outer corner (263) has the
LARGER x, so its ratio is ~ -0.5 when centred while the other eye gives
~ +0.5; the average is ~ 0.0 (-0.0035 on the sample clip) -> every frame is
"Looking Right" (38/38 frames, reported as 108.6%). v1 also ignores head
rotation and vertical gaze.

v2 per eye (both eyes measured in the same image orientation):
  a = image-left corner, b = image-right corner
      image-left eye : a = 33,  b = 133, iris = 468, lids top 159 / bottom 145
      image-right eye: a = 362, b = 263, iris = 473, lids top 386 / bottom 374
  axis = b - a, L = |axis|, n = unit normal of axis pointing image-down
  h = dot(iris - a, axis) / L^2        0 = iris at image-left corner, 0.5 centre
  opening = dot(bottom - top, n)
  v = dot(iris - top, n) / opening      0 = at upper lid, 1 = at lower lid
  open_ratio = opening / L ; open_ratio < gaze.blink_open_ratio (0.12)
      -> blink / closed eye: sample has no gaze reading (not flagged)
  h, v = mean of both eyes (projection on the eye axis also removes roll).

Decision (needs the calibration baseline h0, v0 = medians, §A.4):
  dh = h - h0, dv = v - v0   (centred median over gaze.smooth_window = 3)
  Eye angle from eyeball geometry: iris centre moves r*sin(theta) with
  eyeball radius r ~ 12 mm, visible eye width ~ 30 mm, eye opening ~ 10 mm:
      eye_yaw   = dh * gaze.deg_per_ratio_h   (140 deg per unit ratio)
      eye_pitch = dv * gaze.deg_per_ratio_v   ( 50 deg per unit ratio)
  Head compensation (same sign convention as §A.5, > 0 = image right / down):
      gaze_h = rel_yaw   + eye_yaw
      gaze_v = rel_pitch + eye_pitch
  A screen at ~60 cm spans about +-15 deg horizontally, +-10 deg vertically.
  GAZE_AWAY (raw) if
      (|gaze_h| > gaze.h_thr_deg (22) and |eye_yaw|   >= gaze.min_eye_deg (8))
   or (|gaze_v| > gaze.v_thr_deg (25) and |eye_pitch| >= gaze.min_eye_deg (8))
  The min_eye_deg term means a pure head turn with eyes still on the screen
  (vestibulo-ocular counter-rotation: eye_yaw ~ -rel_yaw) is NOT a gaze
  violation; a head turn with eyes following is caught by HEAD_POSE.
  Persistence: gaze.persist_s = 1.0 s.
  Labels are from the candidate's point of view; with an unmirrored camera
  (gaze.camera_mirrored = false) image right = candidate's LEFT:
      gaze_h > 0 -> "Looking Left", < 0 -> "Looking Right",
      gaze_v > 0 -> "Looking Down", < 0 -> "Looking Up".

Accuracy upgrade (GZ2, 2026-10-14):
  1. Iris centre = mean of the 5 iris landmarks (468-472 / 473-477)
     instead of the single centre point 468 / 473 (less jitter).
  2. Vertical gaze (gaze.vertical = "corner_line"): signed distance of the
     iris centre from the line through the two eye corners, divided by
     the eye width L (positive = below the line = looking down). The
     corners are fixed to the skull; the eyelids follow the eye (the upper
     lid drops when looking down), so the old lid-based ratio
     under-reported downward gaze. Gain from the same eyeball geometry as
     the horizontal ratio (iris shift r*sin(theta) / 30 mm eye width):
     gaze.deg_per_ratio_v = 140 deg per unit ratio. The lids are still
     used for the blink gate (open_ratio).
  3. Eye weighting: h and v = average of both eyes weighted by each eye's
     visible width L. With the head turned, the far eye is foreshortened
     (smaller L) and its ratio is less reliable.
  4. Hysteresis (gaze.hyst_deg = 3): once away, the gaze stays away until
     the combined angle is below threshold - 3 deg (both axes).
  5. Slow baseline adaptation as for head pose: offsets on dh / dv with
     tau = gaze.adapt_tau_s (120 s), only from clearly centred samples,
     capped at gaze.adapt_max_ratio (0.05 = ~7 deg).
  Validation: tests/test_hp2_pose_gaze.py (NOT executed yet).

  L2CS-Net gaze (GZ3, user spec; default gaze.method = "l2cs"):
  Why: the iris-ratio method needs a hand-set gain (deg_per_ratio) and was
  never measured on real "looking away" footage; L2CS-Net regresses the
  gaze direction from the face image (paper: 3.92 deg MAE on Gaze360,
  geometric iris methods typically 8-15 deg).
  Model: L2CS-Net, ResNet50, Gaze360 weights, PyTorch, CPU only.
    Weights: server/models/l2cs/l2cs_gaze360_resnet50.safetensors from
    huggingface.co/py-feat/l2cs = the official L2CSNet_gaze360.pkl converted
    to safetensors by the Py-Feat project (no pickle: loading cannot run
    code). The official Google Drive folder now answers 404. SHA-256
    75405bdc...eca0e checked on download (python -m video.l2cs --download;
    start-backend.ps1 runs it when the file is missing).
    Licence: code MIT; Gaze360-trained weights are for research /
    non-commercial use only.
  Per sample (video/l2cs.py, pipeline._gaze):
    1. MediaPipe landmarks still decide blinks (eye opening < blink ratio
       -> gaze not valid for this sample, as before).
    2. Crop = the candidate's MediaPipe face-detection box (the upstream
       pipeline uses its RetinaFace box), BGR -> RGB, resize 224x224, then
       to the input size, ImageNet normalisation (= upstream pipeline).
    3. Two 90-bin heads (4 deg bins, -180..176): angle = sum(softmax * bin).
       First head = horizontal yaw (> 0 = towards image LEFT), second =
       pitch (> 0 = UP) per the upstream gaze vector (-cos p sin y, -sin p,
       -cos p cos y). Stored as h = -yaw, v = -pitch so > 0 = image right /
       down, the same signs as the iris method (labels: image right =
       candidate's left, camera not mirrored).
    4. Calibration: the candidate-specific forward gaze = median of h / v
       over the accepted calibration samples (same calibration as before).
    5. dh, dv = gaze - baseline (degrees). L2CS gaze already contains the
       head rotation, so head pose is NOT added (gaze.l2cs.min_eye_deg > 0
       can still require an eye-in-head part = gaze - head pose; default 0).
    6. Drift correction: offset follows dh/dv only while |d| < thr/2, tau
       120 s, capped at 5 deg.
    7. Temporal filtering: live = exponential smoothing (alpha 0.5);
       recorded / final = centred moving average over gaze.smooth_window (3).
    8. Away when |dh| > 15 deg or |dv| > 15 deg (spec starting value, tune
       on real sessions); hysteresis 2 deg; then gaze.persist_s (1 s) as before.
  Input size and CPU time (dev machine, after warm-up, 4 torch threads):
    448 (training size)  ~205-240 ms per face -> recorded videos
    224 (Py-Feat size)   ~65-80 ms per face   -> live WebRTC, so the 3 fps
                         budget (333 ms, ~170 ms already used) still holds
    The first passes are ~10x slower (kernel selection): the model runs two
    warm-up passes when loaded and the server loads both sizes at start-up.
  Fallback: if torch / safetensors / the weights are missing, the session
    uses gaze.fallback ("v2" iris method) and the report gets a warning.
  The legacy profile keeps v1's iris method.
  Validation: tests/test_gz3_l2cs.py (decision rule, weights checksum,
    mirror symmetry on the real clip = axis/sign check, smoothing, timing).
    NOT executed (user tests).


A.7 FACE VERIFICATION (V6)
--------------------------
Facts checked in code: keras-facenet's model ends with an l2_normalize
layer and outputs 512-D embeddings (v1 docs said 128-D). With unit vectors
||a-b||^2 = 2 - 2*cos(a,b), so the v1 rule "distance < 1.0" is exactly
"cosine >= 0.5"; the value was never derived from data.

v2:
  1. Detect ALL faces (MediaPipe full-range, conf 0.6, faces narrower than
     face.min_face_px = 40 px ignored). Subject selection (face.select):
       one face        -> that face
       several faces   -> "best_match" (default): embed every face (max 3)
                          and take the one most similar to the reference;
                          if none reaches the threshold, take the LARGEST
                          (ties: most central) and report a mismatch.
     Measured on the sample clip (V6 log): with "largest", an intruder who
     leans in close to the camera becomes the subject (identity score
     -0.015) and his strongly tilted head produced 4 HEAD_POSE false
     positives; the candidate was still there. v1 took the FIRST detection,
     which has the same problem in a random way. "largest" remains
     available as an option.
  2. Bounding box clamped to the image (v1 could produce negative indices,
     which wrap around in numpy slicing).
  3. Crop with face.crop_margin of the box added on every side, resize to
     160x160 (FaceNet was trained on crops with context margin).
  4. score = cosine(embedding, reference). Match if score >= cos_threshold.
     cos_threshold and crop_margin are chosen on PUBLIC data: LFW "train"
     pairs (1100 same / 1100 different person), threshold = max accuracy,
     then reported on the disjoint LFW "test" pairs (500/500). Run:
     tests/calibrate_face_threshold.py. RESULT (LFW test pairs, 500/500):
       crop                         thr    acc    FRR    FAR    AUC
       v1 (first face, no margin)   0.50   0.967  0.062  0.004  0.988
       v2 largest, margin 0.00      0.441  0.979  0.032  0.010  0.996
       v2 largest, margin 0.15      0.432  0.984  0.016  0.016  0.996
       v2 largest, margin 0.30      0.415  0.987  0.014  0.012  0.996
     -> face.crop_margin = 0.30, face.cos_threshold = 0.415 (train max-
     accuracy; the train FAR=1% threshold is 0.413, i.e. the same point).
     FRR (genuine candidate rejected per sample) falls from 6.2% to 1.4%;
     with the 5-sample vote and 2 s persistence a false IDENTITY_MISMATCH
     needs several consecutive rejections. Caveat: LFW is press photos,
     not webcams; MediaPipe found no face in 151 of 6400 LFW images (a
     centre crop was used for those, same for every variant).
  5. Temporal: identity_ok = majority vote over the last face.smooth_window
     = 5 verified samples; IDENTITY_MISMATCH must persist face.persist_s =
     2.0 s. A single bad crop (motion blur, half-turned face) is not a
     mismatch; a different person sitting down for >= 2 s is.
  The face mesh is run with up to face.mesh_max_faces = 3 faces and the mesh
  whose landmark centre is nearest the chosen subject box is used for pose
  and gaze. That centre must lie INSIDE the subject box; otherwise (the
  mesh tracker only found the other person) the sample has no pose/gaze
  reading (pose_ok = false) instead of describing the wrong person.


A.8 MULTIPLE PERSONS (V7)
-------------------------
v1: YOLO person boxes with conf > 0.5 counted; > 1 = multiple. A person
    whose body is hidden (only a face peeking in) or a duplicate box on
    one person (full body + upper body) is miscounted.
v2 (persons.method = "fused"):
  yolo_n  = YOLO person boxes with conf >= persons.yolo_conf (0.5) and
            area >= persons.min_area_frac (1%) of the frame, after
            de-duplication: two boxes are the same person if IoU >=
            persons.dedupe_iou (0.6) or the smaller box lies >= 80% inside
            the larger one.
  face_n  = MediaPipe faces (§A.7 detector, >= 40 px wide).
  persons = max(yolo_n, face_n)
  MAX, not SUM: the same person seen by both detectors is counted once;
  each detector covers the other's misses (face only at the frame edge ->
  face_n; person turned away -> yolo_n).
  MULTIPLE_PERSONS (raw) = persons >= 2, must persist persons.persist_s
  = 1.0 s.

Accuracy upgrade (MP2, 2026-10-14):
  1. Static-face rejection (photos, posters, a face on a screen behind
     the candidate): faces are tracked across samples (match = IoU >= 0.3
     with a face of the previous sample). A track is STATIC when, over its
     last persons.static_samples = 9 samples (3 s at 3 fps), the standard
     deviation of its centre and width is below persons.static_frac =
     0.01 of the face width. A live person always shows micro-motion
     larger than that. Static faces are excluded from face_n and a YOLO
     person box whose only face is static is excluded from yolo_n. The
     subject (verified candidate) face is never excluded.
  2. Face-confirmed second person: YOLO person boxes with confidence
     between persons.yolo_conf_low (0.35) and yolo_conf (0.5) count when
     a (non-static) detected face lies inside the box. This recovers a
     second person who is only partly in view, while low-confidence boxes
     without a face (coats on chairs, shadows) are still ignored.
  3. Persistence k-of-n: the run must cover persons.persist_s (1.0 s)
     and a gap of persons.persist_gap = 1 missed sample is bridged, so one
     detector miss does not split or cancel a visit.


A.9b PROHIBITED OBJECTS ACCURACY UPGRADE (OB2, 2026-10-14)
----------------------------------------------------------
  1. Region-of-interest second pass (objects.roi_pass): when the subject's
     face is visible, a crop around it is analysed again at the model's
     input size: x from face_x - 1.5 w to face_x + 2.5 w, y from
     face_y - 0.5 h to face_y + 4 h (hands, chest, desk), clipped to the
     frame, skipped if it would cover > 60% of the frame. A 1080p frame
     resized to 640 px leaves a phone only ~20-30 px wide; the crop gives
     the same region ~2-3x more pixels. Boxes are mapped back to frame
     coordinates and merged with the full-frame boxes (per class,
     IoU >= 0.5 -> keep the higher confidence). Only prohibited classes
     are taken from the ROI pass (persons always come from the full frame
     so nobody is counted twice). Cost: one extra YOLO call per sample.
  2. Weak detections with a strong anchor: a detection with confidence
     >= objects.weak_conf (0.20) but below the class threshold marks the
     sample "weak". At finalize, a weak sample next to (+-1 sample) a
     confidently detected sample is promoted to detected before the
     2-of-3 persistence. This recovers the first/last frames of an event
     and partly hidden objects without accepting isolated weak hits.
  3. A prohibited-object box lying >= 80% inside the subject's face box is
     ignored (glasses / mouth / nostrils mistaken for a phone).
  The legacy profile keeps v1 behaviour (no ROI pass, no weak promotion).


A.9 PROHIBITED OBJECTS (V8)
---------------------------
Class IDs checked against the loaded model's names at start-up
(yolov8n.pt: 63 laptop, 67 cell phone, 73 book; same as v1). A mismatch
raises an error instead of silently detecting the wrong class.
Per-class confidence (objects.conf): phone 0.35, book 0.40, laptop 0.50
(v1: 0.4 for all). Phones are small and often partly covered by fingers,
so they get the lowest threshold; laptops are large and frequently
confused with monitors/TVs, so they get the highest.
Persistence: PROHIBITED_OBJECT counts when seen in >= objects.persist_k = 2
of objects.persist_n = 3 consecutive samples (a single-sample false
detection is dropped, a one-sample miss inside a run is bridged).
Model size (measured on the labelled sample clip, 12 phone-positive samples,
CPU, median after warm-up):
    yolov8n: phone recall 10/12 (0.833), precision 1.0, YOLO 51 ms/sample
    yolov8s: phone recall 12/12 (1.000), precision 1.0, YOLO 95 ms/sample
  -> objects.weights = "yolov8s.pt" (default). The extra ~45 ms keeps the
  whole sample at ~172 ms, i.e. ~2x faster than real time at 3 fps. The
  test set is small (one clip); yolov8n stays selectable in config (and is
  what the legacy profile uses).
Per-sample output keeps {class name: max confidence} and, for evidence
(§C.5), the boxes {label, conf, xyxy} of every prohibited object found.
Extra screen (EV1): COCO class 62 "tv" (monitors are labelled tv in COCO)
added as "Extra Screen", confidence 0.50. Class ids are verified against
objects.model_names at start-up. The legacy profile keeps the v1 classes.


A.10 FACE NOT VISIBLE + DENOMINATORS (V9)
-----------------------------------------
v1 silently skipped frames without a face, so a candidate who leaves the
camera for 10 minutes produced no evidence at all.
v2: every sample is kept. FACE_NOT_VISIBLE (raw) = no usable face of
    >= 40 px; must persist face_not_visible.persist_s = 2.0 s.
    Scene detectors (persons, objects) still run on no-face samples.
Denominators (also §C). One rule: a sample counts towards a violation type
(numerator AND denominator) exactly when that type was measurable on it.
  HEAD_POSE_DEVIATION, GAZE_AWAY: need the baseline -> analysis-phase
      samples with a visible, measurable face. Calibration samples are
      excluded (nothing to compare against yet).
  IDENTITY_MISMATCH: every sample (calibration included) where a face was
      visible and verified.
  MULTIPLE_PERSONS, PROHIBITED_OBJECT, FACE_NOT_VISIBLE: every sample,
      calibration included.
  Why calibration is NOT excluded for the baseline-free types (changed in
  V6 after a measured loophole): with an impostor on camera no calibration
  sample is accepted, the window extends to 10 s, and excluding it hid the
  impostor for those 10 s (only 7 of 38 samples were scored on the test
  clip). A phone shown during the first 3 s would likewise be ignored.
  (v1 counted gaze/person/object hits during calibration but divided by
  post-calibration frames only, producing 108.6% on the sample clip; here
  numerator and denominator always use the same sample set.)

PART II-B  AUDIO

B.1 AUDIO SOURCES (A1)
----------------------
All audio is converted to 16 kHz, mono, float32 in [-1, 1] before any model.
  Live microphone : sounddevice.InputStream(samplerate=16000, channels=1,
                    dtype=float32), callback appends blocks to a thread-safe
                    buffer; t = samples_received / 16000 (test-start clock,
                    same origin as the video sample timestamps).
  Video file      : audio track decoded with the ffmpeg binary bundled in
                    imageio-ffmpeg:  ffmpeg -i <video> -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000
                    -f f32le pipe:  (stereo -> mono average, resampled by
                    ffmpeg). t = 0 is the first video frame, so audio and
                    video events share one time axis.
                    No audio track -> audio analysis is skipped and the
                    report says "audio: not available" (no audio violations
                    are invented, and audio types are excluded from the
                    score).
  Audio file      : soundfile.read + resampy to 16 kHz when needed (tests,
                    enrollment re-use).
Source status (audio.io.source_status), checked before analysis:
  "unavailable"     no audio track / no samples
  "digital_silence" RMS < audio.silent_rms_dbfs (-80 dBFS): the track exists
                    but carries no signal (muted mic, OS privacy block).
                    Measured: the real sample clip's track is exactly 0
                    (peak 0.000); this machine's mic gave -96.7 dBFS in the
                    smoke test.
  "ok"              otherwise
For "unavailable"/"digital_silence" no audio violations are computed, audio
types are excluded from the score, and the report + UI show
AUDIO_SOURCE_SILENT as a warning for human review (a candidate muting the
mic is itself worth a look, but cannot be told apart from a broken mic).
Recordings never leave the machine; enrollment audio is not stored, only the
voiceprint (§B.3).


B.2 VOICE ACTIVITY DETECTION (A2)
---------------------------------
Primary: Silero VAD (silero-vad package, PyTorch JIT model shipped in the
wheel). Frame = 512 samples (32 ms) at 16 kHz -> speech probability p.
Hysteresis (vad.*):
  speech starts when p >= start_threshold (0.5)
  speech continues while p >= end_threshold (0.35)
  a segment ends after min_silence_ms (300 ms) below end_threshold
  segments shorter than min_speech_ms (250 ms) are discarded
  each segment is padded by speech_pad_ms (100 ms) on both sides
Output: [(start_s, end_s)] speech segments + per-frame probabilities.
Fallback: webrtcvad (aggressiveness vad.webrtc_mode = 2, 30 ms frames) with
the same hysteresis/min-duration rules applied to its 0/1 decisions; used
if the Silero model cannot be loaded. The report records which VAD ran.
Per 0.96 s analysis window (B.4) speech_frac = fraction of the window
covered by speech segments; window is_speech if speech_frac >= 0.5.

Accuracy upgrade (VAD2, 2026-10-14):
  1. High-pass filter before the VAD: 2nd-order Butterworth at
     vad.highpass_hz = 80 Hz, streaming (filter state kept between
     chunks, so live and offline give the same result). Speech has almost
     no energy below 80 Hz; mains hum, desk knocks and handling rumble do.
  2. Probability smoothing: median over vad.prob_median = 3 frames (96 ms)
     before the hysteresis, so one-frame spikes cannot open a segment.
  3. Segment validation (in the audio monitor, needs YAMNet + the signal):
     a. energy gate: segment RMS must be >= noise floor + vad.min_seg_snr_db
        (6 dB); noise floor = 10th percentile of the 32 ms frame RMS over
        the recording. Low enough that faint background voices and
        whispers (typically 10+ dB above room noise) still pass.
     b. short segments (< vad.validate_below_s = 1.0 s) are kept only if
        the YAMNet speech category reaches vad.yamnet_confirm = 0.30 in an
        overlapping window (coughs, clicks, knocks, chair squeaks are short
        and are not speech for YAMNet). Longer segments are trusted to
        Silero. YAMNet whisper >= its threshold also confirms (whispers).
     Rejected segments are counted in the audio result (vad_rejected) and
     are excluded from speech, speaker verification and SPEECH_DETECTED.

Fix (VAD3, 2026-10-14, from the user's live report: 32 segments for ~50 s
of speech, 13 MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS + 5 VOICE_MISMATCH + 5 WHISPERING events
while one person was speaking):
  1. Utterances: validated segments separated by <= vad.merge_gap_s
     (0.8 s) are joined into utterances (natural pauses between words and
     phrases are 0.2-0.6 s). Utterances are used for speaker verification
     and for building audio events (events of the same type closer than
     0.8 s are one event). Speech time / percentages still use the exact
     segments, so no pause is counted as speech.
  2. Speaker chunks must be >= speaker.min_chunk_s = 1.5 s of an utterance
     (v2 allowed a single 1.0 s chunk); utterances shorter than 1.5 s get
     no speaker decision instead of a noisy one (the thresholds were
     calibrated on 2 s chunks).
  3. MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS "accepted + rejected chunk in the same 10 s" rule
     needs a margin of speaker.mix_margin = 0.05 on both sides of
     verify_thr (min < thr - 0.05 and max >= thr + 0.05); similarities
     hovering around the threshold no longer fire. The chunk-pair rule
     (cos < diff_thr) is unchanged.
  4. Whisper voicing cue (§B.4) hardened: the periodicity test now runs on
     70-1000 Hz band-passed audio (the pitch region; removes noise and
     codec artefacts that hid the pitch peak in the browser stream) with
     yamnet.voicing_corr = 0.4; the cue needs voiced_frac <
     yamnet.voiced_frac_whisper = 0.10 and speech_frac >= 0.8 (not a
     window at the edge of speech), and WHISPERING must last
     yamnet.whisper_persist = 3 consecutive windows (~1.5 s).


B.3 VOICE ENROLLMENT (A3)
-------------------------
Happens on the enrollment screen right after the reference photo.
  1. Consent notice is shown: face image + voiceprint are collected for
     proctoring, stored only on this computer (data/enrollment/<id>/), never
     uploaded. Enrollment cannot start until the box is ticked.
  2. A passage is picked at random from audio/passages.py (4 original,
     phonetically varied passages, ~45-55 words, ~15-20 s read aloud).
     Rotating passages makes replaying a single prerecorded clip fail the
     optional ASR check.
  3. Record enroll.record_s = 25 s (16 kHz mono, sounddevice), with a live
     level meter in the UI. (Source status §B.1 checked first: a silent mic
     is reported immediately as "microphone muted or blocked".)
  4. VAD (§B.2) -> voiced segments. Require voiced >= enroll.min_voiced_s
     = 8.0 s, else "please read the whole passage aloud" + retry.
  5. Quality (audio/quality.py), computed on the recording:
       level_dbfs  = RMS of voiced audio
       noise_dbfs  = RMS of non-voiced audio (or 10th percentile of 32 ms
                     frame RMS if there is < 1 s of non-voiced audio)
       snr_db      = level_dbfs - noise_dbfs
       clip_frac   = fraction of samples with |x| >= 0.99
     Fail (retry) if snr_db < enroll.min_snr_db (10), clip_frac >
     enroll.max_clip_frac (0.001 = 0.1%), level_dbfs < enroll.min_level_dbfs
     (-45). Warn (accept) if snr_db < 15 or level_dbfs < -38.
     Each failure has a plain-language message ("too noisy - move somewhere
     quieter", "too loud - move back from the microphone", "too quiet -
     speak up or move closer").
  6. Optional ASR check (enroll.asr_enabled, default false): Vosk small
     English model, offline. word_match = fraction of passage words found
     in the transcript (order-insensitive, lower-case, punctuation
     stripped). Require >= enroll.asr_min_match (0.6). Skipped (not failed)
     when Vosk or its model is not installed; the report records "skipped".
  7. Voiceprint: voiced audio only (VAD segments concatenated), cut into
     enroll.chunk_s = 3.0 s chunks; each chunk -> Resemblyzer d-vector
     (256-D, unit norm); voiceprint = L2-normalised MEAN of the chunk
     vectors. Consistency = mean pairwise cosine between chunk vectors;
     consistency < enroll.min_consistency (0.75) -> retry ("more than one
     voice or heavy noise in the recording").
     Why Resemblyzer, not YAMNet: YAMNet classifies sound events and has no
     speaker identity; Resemblyzer is a speaker encoder (GE2E d-vector),
     runs on CPU in PyTorch next to TF 2.15 without touching the pins, and
     ships its weights in the wheel (no download, fully offline).
     SpeechBrain ECAPA would need torchaudio (+ a HuggingFace download) and
     pins to torch versions that conflict more easily.
  8. Up to enroll.max_attempts = 3 recordings; the best passing attempt is
     kept. Stored: data/enrollment/<id>/voiceprint.npy + meta.json (passage
     id, metrics, attempts, timestamp). The raw recording is NOT stored.


B.4 AUDIO WINDOWS + YAMNET (A4)
-------------------------------
Window = 0.96 s (15360 samples), hop = 0.48 s; window k covers
[0.48k, 0.48k + 0.96). Per window:
  yamnet scores (521 AudioSet classes, model from tfhub.dev/google/yamnet/1,
  cached in models/tfhub so later runs are offline) -> category score =
  MAX over the AudioSet classes mapped to that category:
    category      AudioSet classes                              thr   use
    speech        Speech, Child speech, Narration, Conversation, 0.50  info
                  Babbling, Speech synthesizer, Hubbub (speech  (VAD decides speech)
                  babble). This YAMNet class map has no separate
                  male/female speech classes (checked at load).
    whisper       Whispering                                    0.30  WHISPERING
    phone         Telephone, Telephone bell ringing, Ringtone,  0.30  SUSPICIOUS_SOUND
                  Telephone dialing DTMF, Dial tone, Busy signal
    music         Music, Musical instrument, Singing, Song       0.40  SUSPICIOUS_SOUND
    tv_radio      Television, Radio                             0.30  SUSPICIOUS_SOUND
    typing        Typing, Computer keyboard, Typewriter         0.30  info only
  (yamnet.thresholds in config; set a priori from YAMNet's typical score
  range, NOT tuned on the synthetic test scenes.)
  Persistence (k of n consecutive windows, gaps bridged):
    SUSPICIOUS_SOUND: raw hits separated by <= yamnet.suspicious_gap_s =
    2.0 s are first joined (a ringing phone pauses between rings: real
    cadences are 1-4 s off; on the synthetic 1 s on / 1 s off ring the
    event otherwise split into 3), THEN the run must span >= 2 windows, so
    one isolated window is still dropped. (Order fixed in T2: with "2 of 3
    first, then join", a phone ringing twice in 2.6 s - one 0.90-score
    window per ring, 1.9 s apart - was dropped entirely.)
    WHISPERING 2 of 3.
  Live vs offline: a batch YAMNet frame k actually spans 15600 samples
  (96 STFT frames of 25 ms / 10 ms hop), not 15360; the live path feeds
  exactly that span per window so live and offline scores are identical
  (measured max difference 0.003).
  Whisper, second cue (measured need: on the synthetic whisper scene YAMNet
  gives Whispering 0.000 and Speech 0.99, i.e. YAMNet alone misses it):
  whispered speech has no vocal-fold vibration, so no pitch. For each
  32 ms frame inside VAD speech: voiced if the normalised autocorrelation
  peak in the 2.5-12.5 ms lag range (80-400 Hz pitch) >= 0.5.
  voiced_frac = voiced frames / speech frames in the window.
  WHISPERING (raw) = yamnet whisper >= 0.30
                     OR (window is speech AND voiced_frac < 0.15).
  Caveat: the synthetic whisper test signal was itself made by removing
  voicing, so passing it shows the rule is implemented correctly, not that
  it is accurate on real whispering.
  Speech windows (§B.2): speech_frac >= 0.5 of the window inside VAD
  segments. Typing never creates a violation; it is listed in the report
  as context (a candidate typing is normal in many exams).


B.5 SPEAKER VERIFICATION + MULTIPLE SPEAKERS (A5)
-------------------------------------------------
Runs only on VAD speech (§B.2), never on silence/noise.
  1. Each speech segment is cut into speaker.chunk_s chunks (hop = half a
     chunk; the last partial chunk kept if >= 60% long). Each chunk ->
     Resemblyzer d-vector e_i (256-D, unit norm), time = chunk centre.
  2. Verification: sim_i = cos(e_i, voiceprint). Smoothed = median of the
     chunk and its neighbours within the same segment (speaker.smooth = 3).
     raw VOICE_MISMATCH on a chunk if smoothed sim < speaker.verify_thr.
     VOICE_MISMATCH must persist speaker.persist_s (2.0 s of chunk time).
  3. Multiple speakers (no voiceprint needed, also catches two strangers):
     in every speaker.multi_window_s = 10 s window, if two chunks have
     cos(e_i, e_j) < speaker.diff_thr -> both chunks belong to different
     speakers -> MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS for the span between them. In addition,
     a window holding both an accepted (sim >= verify_thr) and a rejected
     chunk is MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS (candidate + someone else).
  Thresholds are chosen on PUBLIC real speech, Mini LibriSpeech dev-clean-2
  (26 speakers, 16 kHz read speech; tests/calibrate_speaker_threshold.py):
  per speaker a ~20 s voiceprint built exactly like enrollment (§B.3), test
  chunks from the speaker's other utterances; speakers split into a
  calibration half (thresholds) and a held-out half (reported numbers).
  Result (held-out 13 speakers; thresholds from the other 13; EER point;
  tests/results/A5_speaker_calibration.json):
     chunk   verify_thr  FRR    FAR    | diff_thr (1% same-spk  catches diff-
                                       |  pairs below it)       speaker pairs
     1.5 s   0.70        0.2%   1.9%   |  0.567                 85%
     2.0 s   0.72        0.0%   1.9%   |  0.621                 91%
     3.0 s   0.75        0.0%   1.0%   |  0.676                 96%
     (FRR/FAR after median-of-3 smoothing)
  -> speaker.chunk_s = 2.0, verify_thr = 0.72, diff_thr = 0.62: 2 s keeps
     the time resolution of a short remark while staying at ~0% FRR.
  A 10 s window with another voice holds several chunk pairs, so the
  per-pair 91% compounds to a high per-window detection rate.
  Caveats: LibriSpeech is clean audiobook speech; a laptop mic in a room is
  noisier and the threshold may need re-checking on real recordings.

  VOICE2 (user report: voice module gives many false positives). The live
  reports showed the candidate's own voice at similarity 0.60-0.69 (thr
  0.72) and MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS on 25-40% of whole sessions.
  Measured with tests/calibrate_speaker_live.py: real LibriSpeech speech
  through a simulated live channel (enrollment: Opus 64 kbps, 40 dB SNR;
  live: gain 0.5-1.5, noise 12-30 dB SNR, Opus 24/32 kbps; 13 held-out
  speakers): verify_thr 0.72 against the enrollment print alone rejects
  13.1% of the candidate's own 2 s chunks; the pairwise speaker-change rule
  (any pair of chunks within 10 s below diff_thr 0.62) fires on 41% of
  single-speaker windows, because a window holds ~8 chunks = ~28 pairs and
  the minimum of many noisy pair scores is almost always low.
  New rules:
    1. Session print: mean of this session's chunks whose enrollment
       similarity >= speaker.adapt_thr (0.70), if >= 3 such chunks. Each
       chunk's candidate similarity = max(enrollment, session print), then
       median-of-3 per utterance. Without a voiceprint the reference is the
       session's dominant voice. Held-out: FRR 13.1% -> 2.3%, FAR 0.7% ->
       3.3% (EER thr with adaptation 0.724, so verify_thr stays 0.72).
    2. MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS (speaker.multi_rule "foreign_run"): within +-5 s
       a run of >= other_run (3, chunks overlap by 50% -> ~4 s) consecutive
       chunks below other_thr (0.70) AND at least one candidate chunk
       (>= verify_thr); the window must lie on that run. Chunk-level
       calibration: 0% false alarms, 95.7% detection (held-out).
    3. Live check: only when >= 70% of the last 2 s is speech
       (live_min_speech_frac) and with the same session print, built from
       live chunks >= adapt_thr.
  End-to-end (tests/eval_voice2.py, real AudioAnalyzer, 6 held-out
  speakers, share of speech windows flagged VOICE_MISMATCH or
  MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS; old -> new):
    candidate alone (false positives)            23.2% -> 0.0%
    conversation, candidate's own speech         55.6% -> 4.1%
    conversation, other person's speech          99.7% -> 93.3%
    impostor only (VOICE_MISMATCH)               87%   -> 87%
  Caveat: the channel is simulated (Opus + noise + gain); browser noise
  suppression / AGC are not modelled. The old rule stays available
  (multi_rule "pairwise").


B.6 AUDIO EVENTS + TIME ACCOUNTING (A6)
---------------------------------------
Each 0.96 s window k "owns" its central 0.48 s slot
[0.48k + 0.24, 0.48k + 0.72), so overlapping windows never double-count
time (the first window also owns [0, 0.24)). A flagged window adds 0.48 s
of violation time. Audio denominator = analysed audio duration (whole
track / live session; there is no audio calibration phase).
  SPEECH_DETECTED   window is_speech (§B.2) AND policy says so:
                    audio_policy.speech = "flag_after" (default): flagged
                    once cumulative speech time > allow_speech_s = 5 s
                    (a cough or reading a question aloud once is allowed);
                    "flag_all": every speech window; "allow": never (info).
  VOICE_MISMATCH    §B.5 (needs an enrolled voiceprint; without one it is
                    not computed and the report says so)
  MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS §B.5
  WHISPERING        §B.4
  SUSPICIOUS_SOUND  §B.4 (details: which category - phone/music/tv_radio)
Events: consecutive flagged windows of one type are merged into
  {"type", "source": "audio", "start_s", "end_s", "duration_s", "peak",
   "details"} - the same schema as video events (§A.2), on the same clock
  (t = 0 = start of the test / first video frame).
Live (microphone) and offline (file) use the same AudioAnalyzer; in live
mode audio is fed in blocks and YAMNet runs per window as audio arrives,
the final flags/events are computed at the end (finalize), identical to
an offline run of the same audio (tests/test_a4_a6_audio_monitor.py).
Status reported when audio is not analysed: AUDIO_SOURCE_SILENT /
  AUDIO_UNAVAILABLE (§B.1), never counted as a violation.


================================================================================
PART II-C  AGGREGATION, SCORE, VERDICT, REPORT (G1)
================================================================================
Input: video samples (§A) + audio windows (§B), both with final flags and
timestamps on one clock.


C.1 PER-TYPE STATISTICS (explicit numerator / denominator)
  video types (unit = samples; 1 sample = 1/sample_fps seconds):
    denominator = samples on which the type was measurable (§A.10 rule:
                  pose/gaze skip calibration; others count from t = 0)
    numerator   = those samples whose final flag is set
  audio types (unit = seconds, 0.48 s slots, §B.6):
    denominator = analysed audio duration
    numerator   = flagged slots x 0.48 s
  percentage = 100 * numerator / denominator
  count      = number of merged events
  duration_s = flagged time in seconds (video: samples / sample_fps)
  A type that could not be measured at all (no audio, no voiceprint, no
  baseline) is reported with measurable = false and left out of the score.


C.2 VIOLATION SCORE (0-100, points per violation; SC2)
  SC2 (user request): every violation gets its own score with an
  explanation, critical violations weigh heavily, head pose a little,
  eye gaze least. The old weighted MEAN (score = 100 * sum(w*c) / sum(w))
  diluted a critical violation by all the clean types (a full phone
  violation alone gave only ~15 / 100), so it was replaced by a SUM:
    fraction_i = min(1, percentage_i / saturation_i)
    points_i   = max_points_i * fraction_i
    if severity_i = critical and percentage_i > 0:
        points_i = max(points_i, critical_floor * max_points_i)   (0.5)
    score = min(100, sum(points_i))      (unmeasurable types score 0)
  type                severity   max points  saturation%
  IDENTITY_MISMATCH    critical      40          20
  MULTIPLE_PERSONS     critical      35          10
  PROHIBITED_OBJECT    critical      35          10
  VOICE_MISMATCH       critical      30          10
  MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS    critical      30          10
  WHISPERING           critical      20          10
  FACE_NOT_VISIBLE     medium        12          20
  HEAD_POSE_DEVIATION  low           10          25   (v1 threshold)
  SPEECH_DETECTED      low            6          20
  SUSPICIOUS_SOUND     low            6          20
  GAZE_AWAY            very low       4          30   (v1 threshold)
  Saturation = the percentage at which a type earns all its points. The
  critical floor means one confirmed phone / second person / foreign voice
  already costs half its points (17.5 for a phone), enough for SUSPICIOUS.
  Head pose at its full 25% costs 10 and gaze at 30% only 4.
  Each per_violation entry carries score, max_points, severity and
  score_explanation, e.g. "3.2% of samples (12 of 375), 2 event(s). Full 35
  points at 10% or more, so 3.2/10 = 32% of 35 = 11.2, raised to 17.5
  because a critical violation scores at least 50% of its points once it
  occurs. Score 17.5 of 35 points (critical severity)."
  verdict.score_explanation lists the
  sum ("Overall score 27.5 / 100 = sum of the violation points (prohibited
  object 17.5 + head pose deviation 10.0). Score >= 50 -> INVALID, >= 15 ->
  SUSPICIOUS; ...").


C.3 VERDICT
  INVALID if any critical override fires (checked in this priority order,
  the first one is the headline reason; all are listed):
    IDENTITY_MISMATCH  percentage > 50 (v1 majority rule) OR an event
                       >= 10 s -> "Unauthorized person"
    MULTIPLE_PERSONS   percentage >= 20 (v1) OR an event >= 3 s
    PROHIBITED_OBJECT  percentage >= 15 (v1) OR an event >= 3 s
    VOICE_MISMATCH     percentage >= 10 OR an event >= 5 s
    MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS  percentage >= 10 OR an event >= 5 s   (SC2)
  (The event rule is new: on the 12.7 s sample clip a second person is in
  view for ~1.4 s = 12% of samples, below v1's 20%, so v1 said "SINGLE
  PERSON". A 1-hour exam with a 3-minute visitor is only 5%: percentages
  alone hide short critical events.)
  else INVALID if score >= aggregation.invalid_score (50; was 60 for the mean)
  else SUSPICIOUS (review required) if score >= suspicious_score (15; was 20), or
    any CRITICAL type has at least one (persistence-filtered) event below
    the INVALID limits (e.g. a second person for 1 s), or any soft type is
    over its saturation %, or audio_silent_is_suspicious and the audio
    source was silent/unavailable (§B.1).
    (Critical-event rule added in G1: without it a 1 s second person in a
    50 s test scored 2.9 and came out VALID; flicker is already removed by
    persistence, so a remaining critical event deserves a human look.)
  else VALID.
  The legacy profile keeps v1's verdict logic (for regression only).


C.4 OUTPUTS
  JSON report (aggregation/report.py), data/reports/<candidate>_<time>/
    meta        time, candidate, profile, inputs, software versions
    verdict     label, headline reason, all reasons, score,
                score_explanation (SC2)
    per_violation  {type: count, duration_s, percentage, numerator,
                    denominator, unit, severity, max_points, saturation,
                    score, score_explanation, critical, measurable}
    group_percentages  % of the session per evidence group (LIM2, §C.6)
    timeline    all video + audio events sorted by start_s (one clock)
    evidence    grouped evidence package (§C.5), files under evidence/
    evidence_counters / session_violation   (§C.6)
    evidence_upload   per-group upload result (§C.6)
    audio       status, duration, speech_s, VAD engine, voiceprint used
    baseline / calibration info, thresholds used (full config), warnings
  PDF report (PDF1, aggregation/pdf_report.py, reportlab): report.pdf is
    written next to report.json at the end of every session (and rebuilt
    on demand by GET /api/reports/<id>/pdf if missing or older than the
    JSON). Sections: header (candidate, session id, date, mode), verdict
    box (label + icon word, score, all reasons), summary (video / audio /
    events), warnings, session violation + evidence counters with upload
    status, a "Scoring" section (overall score + explanation, then every
    violation with severity, points / max and its explanation; SC2),
    per-violation table (%, numerator / denominator, seconds,
    events, severity, points), percentage bar chart and timeline chart
    (video blue #2a78d6 / audio orange #eb6834, reference palette), the
    evidence groups (head pose, multiple faces, objects: snapshots with
    their captions; the voice clips are NOT included in the PDF / Word
    files (DOCX1, user request) - they stay playable on the results page
    and in evidence/voice/; voice counts still appear in the counters
    and event tables), the full event
    table and the main thresholds used. Only standard PDF fonts and ASCII
    symbols are used (">=" not the Unicode sign) so every viewer renders it.
    A failure while writing the PDF is reported in the console and never
    breaks the JSON report. CLI for existing reports:
      python -m aggregation.pdf_report <report.json> [out.pdf] [--evidence-dir DIR]
    (evidence is looked up next to the JSON, then in data/reports/<id>/).
  Word report (DOCX1, aggregation/docx_report.py, python-docx): same
    sections as the PDF (charts given as tables), evidence snapshots in a
    3-column grid, no voice clips. Built as report.docx on every
    GET /api/reports/<id>/docx. The PDF is now also rebuilt on every
    GET /api/reports/<id>/pdf (no stale copies). CLI:
      python -m aggregation.docx_report <report.json> [out.docx] [--evidence-dir DIR]
  meta.session_id is set for every run (live: WebRTC session id;
  recorded video: job id; CLI: random id) and is used for the upload.
  Console summary: v1-style block plus score, verdict and event list.


C.5 EVIDENCE PACKAGE (EV1, aggregation/evidence.py)
  Built at the end of every session from the final (persistence-filtered)
  events. Four groups, each stored separately and sorted by start time:
    group           violation types                    evidence per event
    voice           VOICE_MISMATCH, MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS,  WAV clip (16 kHz mono) of
                    WHISPERING (voice VIOLATIONS only;  [start - 0.5 s, end + 0.5 s],
                    plain SPEECH_DETECTED is not        max 30 s (evidence.clip_*)
                    evidence - user decision 2026-10-14;
                    it stays in the statistics/timeline)
                    Overlapping or adjacent (gap <= evidence.voice_merge_gap_s
                    = 1.0 s) voice violations are ONE item / clip with
                    "types" listing all of them (v2 saved one clip per type,
                    so one stretch of talking produced 2-3 identical clips).
    object          PROHIBITED_OBJECT                  annotated JPEG snapshot
    multiple_faces  MULTIPLE_PERSONS                   annotated JPEG snapshot
    head_pose       HEAD_POSE_DEVIATION                annotated JPEG snapshot
  Snapshot frame = the "peak" sample of the event that still has a stored
  image (object: highest confidence; multiple faces: most persons; head
  pose: largest |angle|). Images are drawn at analysis time (only for
  samples with a raw violation, bounded by aggregation.max_thumbnails):
  object boxes + "label conf", face boxes + "faces: n / persons: n",
  "dYaw dPitch dRoll" text, timestamp.
  Metadata per item (all groups): id, type, start_s, end_s, duration_s,
  file (relative path), plus
    voice:          speech seconds in the clip, speaker similarity (min) and
                    threshold when a voiceprint exists, yamnet whisper score
    object:         objects [{label, confidence}] at the snapshot, max
                    confidence per label over the event
    multiple_faces: face_count, person_count at the snapshot, max persons
    head_pose:      yaw/pitch/roll (relative, deg) at the snapshot, max
                    |angle| per axis over the event, allowed angles
                    (head_pose.*_thr) and allowed duration (head_pose.persist_s)
  Files: <report dir>/evidence/<group>/<NN>_<type>_<start>s.jpg|wav and
  <report dir>/evidence/<group>/metadata.json.
  Detectable objects (YOLO COCO classes, objects.classes / model_names):
  cell phone (67), book (73), laptop (63), extra screen = tv/monitor (62).
  Earphones are NOT a COCO class: they need a custom-trained detector;
  adding its class id/name/confidence to objects.classes, objects.conf and
  objects.model_names (and the weights to objects.weights) is enough, the
  rest of the pipeline is class-agnostic.


C.6 COUNTERS, SESSION VIOLATION, UPLOAD (EV1, LIM2)
  Counter per group = {count (evidence items), percentage (share of the
  analysed session), basis, limit, limit_text, exceeded}.
  LIM2 (user request): the session limit is a PERCENTAGE of the session,
  not a number of events, except for prohibited objects (any detection):
    group           basis    limit   percentage measured as
    head_pose       percent  25%     HEAD_POSE_DEVIATION % of analysis samples
    multiple_faces  percent   5%     MULTIPLE_PERSONS % of samples
    voice           percent   5%     audio windows with VOICE_MISMATCH or
                                     MULTIPLE_SPEAKERS or WHISPERING (union,
                                     overlaps counted once) x 0.48 s / audio s
    object          count     0      any prohibited-object event
  (evidence.limits in config.py; LIM1's event limits voice 5 /
  multiple_faces 5 became 5% each.) A group is EXCEEDED when its value is
  GREATER than the limit (so "up to" the limit is allowed).
  session_violation.violated = any group exceeded; a violated session is
  never VALID (raised to SUSPICIOUS with the reason "Evidence threshold
  exceeded: head pose (31.0% > 25% of session)", INVALID stays INVALID).
  Live sessions show the same counters while running: count from confirmed
  live alerts (object -> object, person -> multiple_faces, pose ->
  head_pose, voice/whisper -> voice) and a running percentage (confirmed
  samples / samples, head pose after calibration only; voice = audio ticks
  with a confirmed voice or whisper alert / audio ticks); the final values
  come from the report. Reports made before LIM2 keep their count limits
  and are still displayed.
  Upload: for every exceeded group, one package is sent with
    session_id, group, violation summary (candidate, verdict, score,
    counters + thresholds of all groups), the group's metadata and files.
  Target (evidence_upload.url):
    null (default) -> stored by the local backend's evidence store,
                      data/evidence_uploads/<session_id>/<group>/
                      (same code path as the HTTP endpoint)
    "http(s)://.../api/evidence/upload" -> multipart POST (this backend's
                      own endpoint or a central exam server), timeout 30 s.
  The result per group (status uploaded/failed/skipped, target, time,
  error) is written to report.evidence_upload. Uploading evidence to a
  remote URL sends face snapshots and voice clips off this computer; the
  consent text says so, and the default keeps them local.

E.2 LIVE SESSION (webrtc_session.LiveSession)
  Video track: frames are received continuously (aiortc decodes them);
  every 1/sampling.fps s the newest frame (bgr24) is handed to a
  single-thread executor running VideoAnalyzer.process; frames arriving
  while it is busy are dropped (no backlog, the status stays current).
  t = seconds since the first video frame.
  Audio track: av.AudioResampler -> 16 kHz mono s16 -> float; buffered and
  fed to AudioAnalyzer every 0.5 s in a second single-thread executor
  (keeps order). Audio t = samples / 16000 from the first audio frame;
  both tracks start together, so the clocks agree to within the WebRTC
  start-up jitter (a few hundred ms).
  Live flags: LivePersistence over 1 s per monitor; a monitor's confirmed
  count increases when its live flag switches on (these are the alerts).
  Camera black: mean brightness of the first 5 frames < 5 -> warning.
  Mic silent: audio source status (§B.1) after 3 s of audio -> warning.
  Stop: peer connection closed, executors drained, achieved video sample
  rate measured and used for persistence if lower than configured
  (persistence is defined in seconds), VideoAnalyzer/AudioAnalyzer
  finalized, report built and saved (§C.4) with meta: mode live,
  live_sample_rate_hz, camera_black, time per stage.
  Sessions are kept in memory; one server process serves one or more
  sessions, each with its own analyzers (MediaPipe graphs are not shared).
  Performance (PERF1): silero_vad/model.py calls torch.set_num_threads(1)
  at import. That setting is process-wide, so after the first live
  session started YOLO and L2CS ran on ONE thread: live video took ~2 s per
  sample (reports: 0.36-0.64 samples/s instead of 3, video thread busy
  93 s of a 96 s session) and persons / objects / gaze were checked only
  every 2-3 s. audio/vad.py now restores the thread count after loading
  Silero (tests/test_perf1_threads.py guards it). Measured on the real clip
  with the audio analyzer running in parallel: 1080p 752 -> 440 ms per
  sample (YOLO 429 -> 208, mesh + pose + L2CS 235 -> 154); at the live
  640x360 size ~330 ms, i.e. about the 3 fps target. Limiting torch / TF /
  OpenCV thread pools further did not help (~700 ms either way before the
  fix), so the cause was the forced single thread, not over-subscription.

================================================================================
1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The Online Proctoring System is an AI-powered exam monitoring solution that 
combines multiple deep learning models to ensure exam integrity. It performs:

- Identity verification using face recognition
- Adaptive head pose monitoring with baseline calibration
- Eye gaze tracking to detect looking away
- Multi-person detection
- Prohibited object detection (phones, books, laptops)

Technology Stack:
- FaceNet: 128-dimensional face embeddings for identity verification
- MediaPipe: Face detection and 468 facial landmarks
- YOLOv8: Real-time object detection
- OpenCV: Video processing and computer vision operations

================================================================================
3. DETAILED PHASE BREAKDOWN
================================================================================

PHASE 1: INITIALIZATION
-----------------------
Purpose: Load all AI models and prepare system
Duration: ~5-10 seconds (one-time on startup)

Steps:
1. Import libraries (OpenCV, NumPy, MediaPipe, FaceNet, YOLO)
2. Load FaceNet model for face embeddings
3. Initialize MediaPipe Face Detection (confidence: 0.6)
4. Initialize MediaPipe Face Mesh (468 landmarks, refine_landmarks=True)
5. Load YOLOv8 Nano model (auto-downloads if first run)
6. Define 3D face model points for PnP algorithm
7. Set landmark indices for pose estimation
8. Set eye landmark indices for gaze tracking
9. Define prohibited object classes (phone, book, laptop)

Output: All models loaded and ready
Memory: ~500MB-1GB depending on models


PHASE 2: REFERENCE LOADING
---------------------------
Purpose: Create face embedding of authorized person
Input: Reference image file path

Steps:
1. Read reference image from disk
2. Convert BGR to RGB color space
3. Run MediaPipe face detection
4. Extract face bounding box coordinates
5. Crop face region from image
6. Resize face to 160x160 (FaceNet input size)
7. Generate 128-dimensional face embedding
8. Store embedding for comparison

Output: Reference embedding vector (128 floats)
Validation: Raises error if no face detected


PHASE 3: VIDEO INITIALIZATION
------------------------------
Purpose: Prepare video stream for processing
Input: Video file path

Steps:
1. Open video file with cv2.VideoCapture
2. Initialize frame counters (frame_count, processed)
3. Initialize detection counters:
   - same_person_frames = 0
   - different_person_frames = 0
   - deviation_frames = 0
   - gaze_deviation_frames = 0
   - multiple_person_frames = 0
   - prohibited_object_frames = 0
4. Initialize baseline calibration variables:
   - baseline_yaw = None
   - baseline_pitch = None
   - baseline_roll = None
   - baseline_calibrated = False
   - calibration_yaws = []
   - calibration_pitches = []
   - calibration_rolls = []
5. Set MAX_FRAMES = 50 (total frames to process)
6. Set CALIBRATION_FRAMES = 3

Output: Video stream ready, all counters at zero


PHASE 4: BASELINE CALIBRATION (Frames 1-3)
-------------------------------------------
Purpose: Establish user's natural head position as baseline
Duration: First 3 processed frames
Critical: User should maintain natural exam-taking position

Steps for EACH calibration frame:
1. Read frame from video
2. Skip if not every 10th frame (frame_count % 10 != 0)
3. Convert frame BGR to RGB
4. Detect face with MediaPipe
5. Extract bounding box
6. Verify face identity (optional during calibration)
7. Get face mesh landmarks
8. Calculate head pose angles using PnP:
   - Absolute yaw (left/right rotation)
   - Absolute pitch (up/down rotation)
   - Absolute roll (tilt rotation)
9. APPEND angles to calibration lists:
   - calibration_yaws.append(yaw)
   - calibration_pitches.append(pitch)
   - calibration_rolls.append(roll)
10. Log: "Calibrating (X/3)"

After 3rd calibration frame:
1. Calculate baseline as MEAN of collected angles:
   - baseline_yaw = mean(calibration_yaws)
   - baseline_pitch = mean(calibration_pitches)
   - baseline_roll = mean(calibration_rolls)
2. Set baseline_calibrated = True
3. Print baseline values
   Example: "Baseline: Yaw=5.3°, Pitch=110.2°, Roll=-2.1°"

Why Important:
- Handles any starting position (e.g., pitch=110° instead of 90°)
- Eliminates false positives from non-centered users
- Adapts to individual ergonomics
- Baseline becomes the "zero point" for deviation detection


PHASE 5: MAIN PROCESSING LOOP (Frames 4+)
------------------------------------------
Purpose: Analyze each frame for violations
Continues until: processed >= MAX_FRAMES or video ends

Frame Processing Sequence:
1. Read frame
2. Check frame skip (process every 10th)
3. Convert to RGB
4. → Go to SUB-FLOW A: Face Verification
5. → Go to SUB-FLOW B: Head Pose Estimation
6. → Go to SUB-FLOW C: Eye Gaze Tracking
7. → Go to SUB-FLOW D: YOLO Detection
8. Compile and log results
9. Increment counters

Output per frame: Single log line with all detection results


PHASE 6: FINAL DECISION
------------------------
Purpose: Calculate overall verdicts based on statistics
Input: All counter values from processing loop

Steps:
1. Calculate analyzed_frames = processed - CALIBRATION_FRAMES
2. IDENTITY VERDICT:
   IF same_person_frames > different_person_frames
      → "AUTHORIZED PERSON"
   ELSE
      → "UNAUTHORIZED PERSON"

3. DEVIATION VERDICT:
   IF deviation_frames >= 25% of analyzed_frames
      → "DEVIATED"
   ELSE
      → "NOT DEVIATED"

4. GAZE VERDICT:
   IF gaze_deviation_frames >= 30% of analyzed_frames
      → "SUSPICIOUS EYE MOVEMENT"
   ELSE
      → "NORMAL EYE MOVEMENT"

5. PERSON COUNT VERDICT:
   IF multiple_person_frames >= 20% of analyzed_frames
      → "MULTIPLE PERSONS DETECTED"
   ELSE
      → "SINGLE PERSON"

6. OBJECT DETECTION VERDICT:
   IF prohibited_object_frames >= 15% of analyzed_frames
      → "PROHIBITED OBJECTS DETECTED"
   ELSE
      → "NO PROHIBITED OBJECTS"

7. OVERALL VERDICT (Priority order):
   IF "UNAUTHORIZED PERSON"
      → "⚠️ EXAM INVALID - Unauthorized person detected"
   ELSE IF "MULTIPLE PERSONS DETECTED"
      → "⚠️ EXAM INVALID - Multiple persons detected"
   ELSE IF "PROHIBITED OBJECTS DETECTED"
      → "⚠️ EXAM INVALID - Prohibited objects found"
   ELSE IF "DEVIATED" OR "SUSPICIOUS EYE MOVEMENT"
      → "⚠️ EXAM SUSPICIOUS - Review required"
   ELSE
      → "✓ EXAM VALID - No violations detected"

Output: Complete report with all verdicts and statistics

================================================================================
KEY ALGORITHMS & FORMULAS
================================================================================

1. EUCLIDEAN DISTANCE (Face Verification)
------------------------------------------
distance = √(Σ(emb₁ᵢ - emb₂ᵢ)²) for i=1 to 128

Or using NumPy: np.linalg.norm(embedding - ref_embedding)

Threshold: < 1.0 = Same person


2. PERSPECTIVE-N-POINT (Head Pose)
-----------------------------------
Given:
- 3D model points M = [(x₁,y₁,z₁), ..., (x₆,y₆,z₆)]
- 2D image points I = [(u₁,v₁), ..., (u₆,v₆)]
- Camera matrix K

Find rotation R and translation T such that:
I ≈ K[R|T]M

Using cv2.solvePnP with ITERATIVE method


3. EULER ANGLES FROM ROTATION MATRIX
-------------------------------------
Given rotation matrix R:

sy = √(R[0,0]² + R[1,0]²)

pitch = atan2(R[2,1], R[2,2]) * 180/π
yaw   = atan2(-R[2,0], sy) * 180/π
roll  = atan2(R[1,0], R[0,0]) * 180/π


4. GAZE RATIO CALCULATION
--------------------------
eye_width = |inner_corner_x - outer_corner_x|

gaze_ratio = (iris_center_x - outer_corner_x) / eye_width

Range: [0.0, 1.0]
- 0.0-0.35: Looking away (right)
- 0.35-0.65: Looking at screen (center)
- 0.65-1.0: Looking away (left)


5. BASELINE CALCULATION
------------------------
After collecting N calibration frames:

baseline_yaw = mean([yaw₁, yaw₂, ..., yawₙ])
baseline_pitch = mean([pitch₁, pitch₂, ..., pitchₙ])
baseline_roll = mean([roll₁, roll₂, ..., rollₙ])

Relative angles:
Δyaw = current_yaw - baseline_yaw
Δpitch = current_pitch - baseline_pitch
Δroll = current_roll - baseline_roll


6. THRESHOLD CALCULATIONS
--------------------------
For each violation type with threshold T%:

violation_threshold = T * analyzed_frames

Example: Deviation threshold at 25%
- analyzed_frames = 47
- threshold = 0.25 * 47 = 11.75 frames
- If deviation_frames >= 12 → DEVIATED

================================================================================
ERROR HANDLING & EDGE CASES
================================================================================

1. No face in reference image
   → Program exits with error message

2. No face detected in video frame
   → Skip frame, continue to next

3. Face too small/far
   → May skip (bbox validation)

4. Multiple faces in frame
   → Uses first detected face for identity
   → Counts all faces for violation

5. Poor lighting
   → May reduce detection accuracy
   → Confidence thresholds help filter

6. Baseline calibration failure
   → Would proceed without baseline
   → Fall back to absolute angles
   → Warning in final report

7. Video file not found
   → OpenCV error, program exits

8. Insufficient frames in video
   → Process what's available
   → May not reach MAX_FRAMES
